    Measurements and how to read the chart

    Each benchmark is normalized to the full measured cohort’s fewest source lines and fastest median elapsed time. Hiding a language or program does not move those baselines. Axis limits use the full admitted cohort for the selected benchmark set and remain fixed when languages, profiles, programs, or standard-deviation ellipses are toggled. Changing the benchmark set, chart view, or Focus on means setting establishes a new range. Hiding Samples restores the means range and removes the option to fit hidden data points. Selecting benchmarks recomputes equal-weight geometric means from that subset.

    Circles are the fastest included programs; squares are those with the fewest LOC. A line joins the two profiles. Faint filled marks are their benchmark medians. Samples independently shows small hollow dots for actual recorded runs at their source LOC and elapsed-time ratio to the benchmark’s fixed baseline, including when both profiles and the global median are hidden. The normal comparison uses the admitted endpoint pool for each language and benchmark. Clicking a mean lets you inspect its contributing programs. Clicking a language name opens its overview: all ten workloads remain listed, every program in the publication’s qualified inventory and every recorded run is available, and missing coverage is labelled. Diamonds and covariance contours summarize the language’s benchmark endpoints; the table reports each program’s separate run-to-run SD.

    Density map smooths the enabled geometric-mean profile positions on the entire fixed log₂ chart domain. Each selected execution variant has total weight one, split equally across its distinct enabled positions; coincident fastest/fewest-LOC positions contribute once. A Gaussian kernel is sampled on a 48 × 36 grid with axis bandwidths of 1.25 grid cells, stated in log₂ doublings in the legend. Colors use square-root-scaled intensity relative to the current selection’s peak, rather than an absolute probability scale. Year, trait, language, benchmark and program selections recompute the population. Recorded runs remain an independent Samples overlay and do not add density weight.

    Global median & SD highlights the coordinate median of the visible fastest and fewest-LOC endpoints, fading profile means while recorded samples stay independently visible. White denotes fastest; gold denotes fewest LOC. Inner contours show 1σ covariance in log₂ LOC/time space, with a lighter 2σ contour. Covariance is computed about the log-space mean and its contours are translated to the median, preserving their shape and area. Each visible execution variant has equal weight. These describe variation between entries, not timing uncertainty. At least two entries are required for covariance. The median trail follows cumulative cohorts within the selected inclusive release-year window and current benchmark, language and program selections. Play window steps through matching published release years between the two handles and enables the median overlay. Pause freezes its cutoff; adjusting either handle or choosing a decade selects a new window. Baselines and axes stay fixed throughout playback.

    Language checkboxes group runtime variants: Mech has AOT, JIT and interpreted execution; Lua has interpreted Lua and LuaJIT; C# and F# each have JIT and AOT; Dart has AOT and JIT. Child checkboxes keep each variant independently selectable, with separate measured endpoints and means. A partially checked parent means some variants are enabled. Lua has nine admitted workloads and no recorded pi-digits endpoint; its partial coverage is labelled, with no invented timing.

    The comparison table shows each language’s fewest-LOC multiple and fastest-time multiple relative to 1×. On the full chart these are equal-weight geometric means over the compared benchmarks. A 1.46× LOC value means 46% more lines than the shortest-source baseline. These two best values can come from different programs. For the line from the square to the circle, Δx = log₂(fastest LOC / fewest LOC) and Δy = log₂(fastest time / fewest-LOC time). Slope = Δy/Δx; length = √(Δx² + Δy²), in doublings rather than screen pixels. Coincident endpoints have zero length and undefined slope.

    The release-year window includes both selected endpoints. Earlier languages are excluded from the visible comparison and its median history. Playback steps only through matching years inside that window. Mech uses 2026 for the version under test. Language years and their sources are listed in the controls. This changes visibility only: benchmark samples, geometric-mean inputs, normalization baselines and axis limits stay tied to the selected benchmark cohort.

    Hovering a language’s point or line shows its covariance contours; each oval button keeps them visible. Contours use log₂ LOC/time space, separately for the fastest and fewest-LOC profiles. Inner contours are 1σ; lighter outer contours have twice the radius along both covariance axes. Centers are the geometric means and tilt preserves LOC/time correlation. The sortable 2σ area columns report 4π√det(Σ), using sample covariance across the selected benchmarks, in squared doublings. They measure each full ellipse before display clipping and remain available when its contours are hidden. With fewer than two benchmarks the area is undefined (—); a degenerate ellipse has zero area. These statistics describe variation across benchmarks, not timing uncertainty or a confidence interval. The code panel separately shows run-to-run timing deviation. Focus on means fits the axes to the full cohort’s geometric-mean endpoints, cropping outlying samples and contours. While Samples is enabled, uncheck it to fit benchmark samples and complete covariance envelopes. Hiding Samples restores Focus on means and removes the full-data fit option; showing Samples again retains the means range until you change it. In either mode, visibility controls keep the limits fixed. The first labelled tick on each axis is always the 1× measured baseline; covariance tails below 1× are clipped without changing the computed means or covariance.
